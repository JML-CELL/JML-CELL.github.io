Knowing how long someone brushes does not reveal whether each tooth surface receives attention. LiT monitors 16 brushing surfaces using the light from a commercial blue-LED toothbrush and two inexpensive photosensors on the brush head. The design models light transmission inside the mouth to determine sensor placement and connect optical patterns with brushing locations.

The processing pipeline separates brushing actions and addresses interference around the front teeth and variation between users. Experiments with 16 users report 95.3% recognition accuracy and a 6.1% brushing-duration estimation error. LiT demonstrates a compact optical approach to obtaining more detailed feedback from a familiar everyday object.
