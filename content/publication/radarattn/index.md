Radar activity-recognition models must capture complex motion patterns without making inference unnecessarily expensive. RadarAttn uses visual attention to emphasize informative regions of radar spectrograms and self-attention to model longer-range relationships in the signal. These two mechanisms address complementary aspects of feature selection.

Evaluation on radar activity-recognition benchmarks considers accuracy together with parameter count and floating-point operations. The contribution is an attention-based architecture that treats computational efficiency as part of the recognition problem, supporting more practical deployment of radar sensing.
