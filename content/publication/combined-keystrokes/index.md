When two keys are pressed together, their acoustic responses mix and can no longer be matched directly to isolated-key recordings. The system uses blind signal separation to disentangle the mixture, followed by feature extraction and pattern recognition to identify the constituent keystrokes.

Laboratory experiments use mobile phones with two microphones and evaluate combinations of two keys, reporting 78.4% average recognition accuracy. The work demonstrates a route from single-key acoustic recognition to simultaneous inputs and examines the role of signal separation in that transition.
