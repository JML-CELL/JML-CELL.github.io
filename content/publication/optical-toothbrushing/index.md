Brushing quality depends on how attention is distributed across the mouth, not just on total brushing time. This study investigates optical sensing as a way to observe brushing behavior, linking measured light patterns with the movement and position of a toothbrush during oral care.

The work focuses on an intelligent monitoring system that converts physical measurements into more informative feedback. Its research question is how optical observations can support detailed monitoring in a compact everyday device, where sensing conditions change with the user and the brushing action.
