Partially overlapping channels do not necessarily destroy every symbol in an OFDM packet. Piros examines the remaining coding redundancy and extracts usable information even from symbols affected by adjacent-channel interference. Recovery decisions take the amount of channel overlap into account.

The short paper introduces a distributed way to exploit this partial decodability. Its contribution is to reconsider a collision as structured damage that may be recoverable, opening opportunities for concurrent Wi-Fi transmissions on channels traditionally kept separate.
