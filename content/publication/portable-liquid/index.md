Different liquids resist motion differently, allowing active smartphone vibration to provide information about viscosity. The system combines a physical model with signal-processing stages that address low sampling rates, the phone’s own vibration, and changes in liquid volume. It reconstructs useful motion information rather than relying solely on a learned classifier.

The journal study reports a mean relative viscosity-estimation error of 2.3% and 97.33% average recognition accuracy across 30 liquids. The contribution is a practical measurement pipeline that brings liquid identification to a readily available device.
