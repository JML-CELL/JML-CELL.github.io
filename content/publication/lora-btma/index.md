In a LoRa network, two transmitters may be unable to hear one another while still interfering at a common receiver. This hidden-terminal condition is difficult to handle using local channel sensing alone. The paper studies a busy-tone multiple-access approach that uses an additional channel-occupancy indication to support access coordination.

The research focuses on the MAC layer: how to turn information about ongoing communication into better transmission decisions. It examines collision avoidance as a coordination problem, complementing physical-layer techniques that improve the reception of individual LoRa signals.
