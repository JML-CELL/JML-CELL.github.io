This journal study develops ViType around a single small vibration sensor operating at a relatively low sampling rate. Fine-grained feature extraction makes use of the limited signal information, while runtime calibration and adaptation address changes in tapping behavior and signal characteristics over time.

Experiments with 30 participants examine recognition under confounding factors and over a month of use. The work connects low hardware complexity with ongoing adaptation, showing how an on-body input interface can remain useful without relying on a large array of high-rate sensors.
