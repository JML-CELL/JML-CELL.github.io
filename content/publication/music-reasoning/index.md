Symbolic music can be encoded as discrete tokens, but knowing musical terminology is different from applying it through several reasoning steps. This study evaluates large language models on advanced music understanding and conditioned generation, focusing on song-level tasks that matter for editable, interactive composition.

An analysis of model responses identifies weaknesses in multi-step reasoning and in applying previously learned musical knowledge to complex tasks. The paper contributes an evaluation perspective for human–AI music co-creation: performance should be judged by musical constraints and reasoning behavior, not solely by the apparent fluency of generated text or notation.
