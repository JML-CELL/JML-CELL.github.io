Mobility changes the interference relationships between wireless subnetworks, while collecting every instantaneous channel gain is impractical. The method therefore works with per-channel received signal strength. Its GA-Net architecture combines hard attention, which filters less relevant neighboring subnetworks, with graph attention to represent the relationships that matter for resource allocation.

Multi-agent reinforcement learning uses these representations to coordinate radio-resource decisions. The contribution is a resource-management design that works from aggregate measurements rather than requiring a complete instantaneous channel map, addressing both limited observability and interference coupling in mobile subnetworks.
