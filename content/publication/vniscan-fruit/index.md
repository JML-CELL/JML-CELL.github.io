Conventional soluble-solids measurements often require extracting juice or using costly spectrometers. VNiScan-Fruit instead builds a visible–near-infrared sensing unit from commercial LEDs and photodetectors. A ring-shaped enclosure, interference removal, and spectral reconstruction help recover useful reflectance features despite differences in fruit peel and surface texture.

A nonlinear regression model maps these features to soluble solids content, using refractometer measurements as references. Tests cover fruits with different peel characteristics and changing lighting and temperature. The reported normalized mean absolute error is 8%, demonstrating the potential of compact optical hardware for non-destructive fruit-quality assessment.
