Conventional LoRa backscatter designs use full-length chirps even when the channel could support a shorter transmission. TagEcho addresses this inflexibility by varying symbol duration, balancing reliability against throughput as wireless conditions change. A frequency-hopping mechanism embeds control information, while multi-chirp and sub-chirp modes provide two ways to adapt modulation without disrupting the signal’s temporal structure.

The authors build a hardware prototype and evaluate indoor and outdoor links against TagLite. Under the reported test conditions, the prototype supports communication over 3.1 km and reaches a single-tag throughput of 3,906 bps. The contribution is a practical connection between low-energy backscatter hardware, control signaling, and adaptive communication.
