Color converters in visible-light communication must emit useful light while responding quickly enough to carry data. This study tests aggregation-induced emission luminogens in solid-powder form, examining emission color, electrical modulation bandwidth, and achievable data rates. Their behavior at high concentration makes them relevant to practical optical components.

The analysis relates converter lifetime to modulation bandwidth and photoluminescence efficiency to signal-to-noise ratio. By connecting material measurements with communication performance, the paper provides guidance for selecting and improving color-conversion materials rather than treating the optical material and data link as separate problems.
